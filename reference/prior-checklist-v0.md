# Ballot Measure Analysis — Concerns Checklist
## Multi-Angle Evaluation Framework

Use this checklist when reading the **full official text** of a ballot measure (not the title, summary, or voter-guide arguments). Flag exact clauses that map to the categories below. Quote the language, state the factual implication, and note which concern it primarily triggers.

The goal is to surface trade-offs so users can see what each side is prioritizing or conceding.

---

## 1. Conservative / MAGA-Leaning Concerns

### Power Expansion & Delegation
- New agencies, boards, commissions, or offices created
- “Shall adopt regulations,” “implementing regulations,” or broad rule-making authority
- Power shifted from elected officials to unelected staff, experts, or advisory bodies
- Private rights of action or standing granted to advocacy organizations

**Red-flag phrases**: “as necessary,” “consistent with the purposes of this measure,” “the department shall,” “notwithstanding any other provision of law”

### Fiscal Responsibility & Taxpayer Exposure
- New taxes, fees, or bonds
- Continuous appropriation or automatic funding
- Escalators, cost-of-living adjustments, or open-ended spending
- Weak or missing sunset clauses
- Spending that does not require future voter approval for expansion

**Red-flag phrases**: “continuously appropriated,” “adjusted annually,” “as determined by,” “without further appropriation”

### Election Integrity & Process
- Changes to voter identification, registration, mail voting, or ballot counting rules
- Changes to recall, referendum, or initiative processes
- Shifts in who controls election administration

### Parental Rights & Education
- Curriculum mandates
- Gender identity or pronoun policies
- Parental notification or opt-out rules
- State overrides of local school board decisions

### Crime, Public Safety & Criminal Justice
- Changes to sentencing, bail, policing standards, or liability
- Expansions or contractions of enforcement authority
- New private rights of action related to public safety

### Local Control vs. State Preemption
- State rules that override local zoning, housing, energy, education, or land-use decisions
- “Preempts local ordinances” or similar language

### Business, Property & Economic Freedom
- New regulations or liability expansions on businesses or property owners
- Restrictions on energy production, contracting, or land use
- Changes to classification of workers or contractors

### Free Speech & Association
- Compelled speech or disclosure requirements
- Restrictions on association or contracting based on viewpoints
- Private rights of action usable against individuals or organizations for speech-related claims

---

## 2. Liberal / Progressive-Leaning Concerns

### Equity, Disparate Impact & Protected Classes
- Explicit or expansive definitions involving race, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, or socioeconomic status
- Data collection or reporting requirements tied to these categories
- Affirmative obligations or “equity” mandates on public or private entities

**Red-flag phrases**: “equity,” “disparate impact,” “gender identity,” “historically marginalized,” “inclusive”

### Climate, Environment & Environmental Justice
- New emissions standards, review processes, or agency powers
- “Environmental justice” or equity language applied to permitting or land use
- Expansions of environmental review that can be used for non-environmental purposes

### Healthcare Access & Public Health
- Coverage mandates or expansions
- New authority for public-health agencies
- Requirements on providers, insurers, or facilities

### Housing & Tenant Protections
- Rent control, just-cause eviction, or inclusionary zoning rules
- State overrides of local housing or zoning decisions
- Expansions of tenant rights or landlord liability

### Labor & Worker Protections
- Minimum standards, classification rules, or union-related provisions
- Private rights of action for workers
- Requirements on contractors or employers

### Campaign Finance & Political Process
- Public financing of campaigns
- Contribution limits, disclosure rules, or matching funds
- Changes to how elections or recalls are funded or administered

### Civil Rights Enforcement & Standing
- Expanded private rights of action
- Standing granted to organizations or classes of people
- New enforcement mechanisms or penalties

### Data Privacy & Surveillance
- New collection, sharing, or use of personal data by government or private parties
- Mandates that create databases usable for later regulation

---

## 3. Shared / Cross-Cutting Concerns

These appear in measures favored by either side. Flag them regardless of the measure’s primary sponsor.

- **Vague or expansive definitions** — Key terms defined so broadly they can be interpreted later to cover new situations.
- **Future flexibility / preemption language** — “Notwithstanding any other law,” or clauses that override existing limits.
- **Accountability & oversight gaps** — New powers with limited legislative, judicial, or voter checks; heavy reliance on future regulations.
- **Implementation risk** — Real scope depends on agency interpretation, litigation, or subsequent rules rather than the text itself.
- **Trade-offs** — What one side gains and what the other side is required to accept.

---

## 4. Historical Pattern Notes (for context, not automatic scoring)

Use these only to illustrate how similar language has been applied in the past. Always ground the analysis in the current measure’s actual text.

- Broad “equity” or “disparate impact” language has been used to expand data-collection and policy mandates beyond the original ballot description.
- “Implementing regulations” authority has allowed agencies to fill in operational details that significantly shaped the measure’s real-world effect.
- Continuous appropriation or weak sunset language has led to spending growth without new voter approval.
- Private rights of action have enabled litigation that expanded the practical scope of a measure through settlements or court rulings.
- State preemption clauses have overridden local decisions in housing, education, and environmental policy.

---

## How to Apply This Checklist

1. Obtain and quote from the **full official text**.
2. Identify clauses that match any category above.
3. For each flag: quote the language → state what it factually does or enables → note which concern category it primarily triggers → note any clear trade-off for the other side.
4. Produce a neutral summary of the powers and obligations actually created by the text.
5. Only after the factual summary, highlight the strongest points under a specific ideological lens if the user requests it.

The output should let readers from either side see the concrete implications rather than only the framing that matches their priors.
